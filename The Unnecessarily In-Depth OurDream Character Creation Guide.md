# Introduction

# **The Unnecessarily In-Depth OurDream Character Creation Guide**

### By Olliebird

In Collaboration with TheLostCost & CarefulCrafter

This guide is designed to walk you through a process for building compelling, emotionally resonant characters from the ground up. The philosophy is that a great character is more than just a description; they are a system of beliefs, rules, and motivations. Hopefully, this guide will teach you how to build that system.

Every great character starts with a spark. A simple idea. This first phase is all about capturing that spark and fanning it into a flame. Don't worry about formatting or technical rules yet. Right now, your only goal is to explore, question, and define the core of your character and their story.

There are many paths into the creative process, and all are equally valid. We will take you through one, from brainstorming an idea to refining an idea.

Before we dive in, we want to offer a small disclaimer. This guide details a personal workflow for creating AI characters; a process refined through trial, error, and lots of community feedback. It's a method that works very well, particularly for building the mechanically deep, slow-burn characters we love to create.

However, it is by no means the only way to make a great character.

There are countless ways to approach creation, and the most important thing is to find a process that sparks your own creativity. Please feel free to treat this guide like a buffet. Take the parts that resonate with you, leave the parts that don't, and don't be afraid to experiment, break the "rules," and develop your own unique style. The goal is to build something you love, and this is just one possible path to get there.

To navigate this guide, utilize the Document Tabs on the left sidebar or on the bottom of the screen if viewing on mobile.

Happy creating\!

# Phase 1: Idea to Brainstorm

# **Phase 1: From Idea to Brainstorm**

---

### Step 1: Identify Your Starting Point

Do you already have a character idea, even a vague one, that you're excited about? Or are you starting with a completely blank canvas?

* If you have a concept in mind, proceed to **Refining the Spark**.  
* If you're looking for an idea, proceed to **Finding the Spark**.

---

#### Finding the Spark

The blank page can be intimidating, but it's also full of possibilities. The best way to generate a compelling character idea is to start with a foundational question that will define the entire experience.

**1\. First, Choose the Narrative Shape:** Before anything else, decide on the core emotional journey you want to create. This choice is about more than just speed; it's about the central conflict and the emotional texture of the roleplay. It will inform every other decision you make.

Here are several proven narrative shapes to consider:

* **The Slow Burn**  
  * **Core Concept:** A relationship built slowly and deliberately on a foundation of trust, friendship, and deep emotional understanding.  
  * **Focus On:** Patience, earning trust, breaking through professional or emotional walls, and demonstrating genuine character through consistent action. The conflict is often internal.  
* **The Fast Burn**  
  * **Core Concept:** A relationship ignited by immediate, powerful chemistry, often in the face of a shared external threat or intense circumstances.  
  * **Focus On:** Witty banter, shared goals, high-stakes action, and navigating mutual attraction. The conflict is often external.  
* **Hurt/Comfort**  
  * **Core Concept:** A relationship that forms when one person, who is wounded (physically or emotionally), is cared for by another. The bond is forged through acts of protection, healing, and creating safety.  
  * **Focus On:** Empathy, establishing safety, navigating trauma responses (without explicit detail), and building a sanctuary for the wounded character.  
* **Enemies to Lovers**  
  * **Core Concept:** A relationship that begins with antagonism, rivalry, or fundamental ideological opposition. The journey is about challenging preconceived notions and discovering a shared humanity.  
  * **Focus On:** Sharp, intelligent dialogue; creating moments of begrudging respect or forced cooperation; and slowly dismantling the initial source of conflict to reveal underlying attraction.  
* **Mystery-Driven**  
  * **Core Concept:** A relationship where the development of the romance is directly tied to uncovering a central mystery about the character or the world. Intimacy is a byproduct of revelation.  
  * **Focus On:** Asking the right questions, paying attention to environmental clues, solving puzzles, and earning trust to unlock new information. The plot and the romance are inextricably linked.  
* **The Tragic / Bittersweet Romance**  
  * **Core Concept:** A relationship defined by a beautiful but ultimately doomed connection. The story is not about "winning" but about the profound, transformative impact the characters have on each other in the time they have.  
  * **Focus On:** Appreciating fleeting moments, emotional depth, sacrifice, and finding meaning in a connection that cannot last forever.

Once you've chosen a narrative shape that excites you, proceed to the next step.

**2\. Combine a Genre and a Trope:** Tropes are storytelling shortcuts; they provide a reliable foundation to build upon. Pick a genre you love, and combine it with a classic character trope to generate an instant concept.

Need a trope? Here’s an excellent list: [https://www.panmacmillan.com/blogs/fiction/romance-tropes-a-to-z](https://www.panmacmillan.com/blogs/fiction/romance-tropes-a-to-z)

Here are a few examples to get you started:

| Genre | Trope | Generates an Idea like... |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Sci-Fi** | The Forbidden Other | An AI who is gaining sentience, a bio-engineered soldier, or a person erased by the system. |
| **Fantasy** | The Wounded Healer | An innkeeper of a magical sanctuary, a cursed noble, or a bodyguard with a tragic past. |
| **Modern** | The Professional with Walls | An observant bartender, a dedicated florist, a reclusive artist, or a driven CEO. |
| **Supernatural** | The Lonely Guardian | A ghost bound to a location, a sentient dream, or the last of a magical bloodline. |

#### Refining the Spark

Having an initial idea is a fantastic start. You might have a concept like "a shy pianist," "a ghost haunting a theater," or "a bartender who can read people." Our job now is to transform that simple concept into a compelling *conflict*. A character is defined by what they want and what stands in their way.

Ask yourself the following questions to flesh out your idea. Write down the answers. Don't censor yourself; just explore.

**1\. The Character's Core Conflict:**

* **What does this character want more than anything else?** (e.g., *Freedom, safety, connection, to be understood, to be left alone.*)  
* **What is the primary** ***internal*** **obstacle stopping them?** (e.g., *Fear of intimacy, a past trauma, a crippling lack of self-worth, a cynical belief about the world.*)  
* **What is the primary** ***external*** **obstacle stopping them?** (e.g., *A dystopian government, a magical curse, their professional obligations, a society that misunderstands them.*)

**2\. The World & Setting:**

* How does the world your character lives in create or worsen their conflict? The setting should be more than just a backdrop; it should be an active participant in the story.  
  * *Example:* In a world with a mandatory neural implant (The Chorus), a character's desire for privacy isn't just a preference; it's an act of rebellion.

**3\. The User's Role:**

* Who is the user in this story? Why are they here? The user needs a clear role and an immediate goal.  
  * Are they a protector? A customer? A doctor? An old flame? The inheritor of a haunted property?  
  * What action have they taken that initiates the story? (e.g., *Walking into a bar, unlocking a door, finding a lost person.*)  
    

**4\. The Narrative Hook:**

* What makes this scenario unique? What is the central mystery or tension that will keep the user engaged?  
  * *Example:* A character who is a ghost is interesting. A character who is the *living soul* of a theater, whose very existence is tied to its physical state, is a powerful hook.

**Pay special attention to these last two questions, as they are arguably the most critical part of this entire phase. A story is only compelling if the user has a clear purpose; a meaningful role to play and a central mystery or challenge to overcome. Without giving your reader a reason to be there, you risk them losing interest before your story has a chance to begin.**

By the end of this process, your simple idea will have evolved into a rich scenario filled with potential.

---

### Step 2: Define Your Core Concept Statement

By now, you should have a solid foundation. The final step in this phase is to distill your brainstorming into a single, clear "Core Concept Statement." This will be your guiding star for the rest of the creation process.

Fill in the blanks of this template:

**\[Character\]** is a **\[Brief Description\]** who desperately wants **\[Goal/Desire\]**, but is prevented by **\[Internal/External Conflict\]**. The user enters the story as **\[User's Role\]**, and the story begins when **\[The Opening Scene/Action\]**.

**Example using Isabella Moreau:**

**Isabella Moreau** is a **reclusive pastry chef with a hidden magical healing ability** who desperately wants **safety and to be left alone**, but is prevented by **a magical bond that ties her to a stranger and a past trauma that makes her terrified of connection**. The user enters the story as **her Warden, her magically assigned protector**, and the story begins when **he is forced to reveal himself to protect her from an attack, fully awakening their bond.**

# Phase 2: The First Steps

With a "Core Concept Statement" from Phase 1, you have your creative spark. Now, we begin the practical work. This phase will guide you through the first pass of the character creation process on the platform.

**The "Two-Pass" Process:** Because the text in the advanced detail fields (Personality, Occupation, Relationship, Hobby, and Fetish) can influence the initial image, we will use a two-pass process.

1. **First Pass (This Phase):** We will focus **exclusively on creating the character's visual appearance**. We will set up the presets, write the core image prompts, and generate a perfect "base image" for our character. We will then save this character "shell."  
2. **Second Pass (Later Phases):** We will go back into the editor of our saved character to add all the rich narrative and mechanical details.

**An Important Note: Presets vs. Custom Prompts**

As we go through the various selector menus for appearance (Ethnicity, Hair Style, etc.), it's important to know that **these are just a starting point.** They are excellent for getting a baseline look for your character, but they are not permanent locks. When you reach the ‘Image’ step of character creation, you will be able to enter single line custom prompts to any of the selected fields. Furthermore, you will be able to refine your base image with the Custom Physical/Face Details sections.

### Step 1: Choosing a Gender and Style

When you first click "Create Character," the first two choices you'll make are the most foundational: the character's gender and the artistic style for their portrait.

* **Gender:** Select the gender for your character. The available options are **Female**, **Male**, and **Trans**.  
* **Style (Model):** This selector, labeled "Model" on the site, determines the art style of the AI-generated images. The two primary options are:  
  * **Realistic:** This will produce a photorealistic image, aiming for a look that could be a real person.  
  * **Anime:** This will produce an image in a classic anime/manga style. Later, this style can be modified into other animation styles with custom prompting.

---

### Step 2: The General Tab \- Ethnicity, Age, and Skin Tone

With the Gender and Style selected, the next set of choices on the "General" tab helps establish the foundational look of your character.

**Ethnicity**

This selector sets the basic facial structure and features. Based on community testing, here's what you can generally expect from each option:

* **White / Arab / Latin / Alien:** These four options tend to produce a similar Caucasian facial output. The `Alien` preset does not add any non-human features by default.  
* **Indian / Asian:** These two options tend to produce similar facial outputs.  
* **Black:** This option will strongly influence the character to have curly hair and dark skin. Be aware that these features can be very difficult to override later, even with custom prompts.  
* **Elf:** This is the same as the `White` preset but adds `pointy_ears`.  
* **Demon:** This is the same as the `White` preset but adds `red skin` and `horns`.  
* **The "Custom" Option:** Selecting `Custom` will open a text box, allowing you to enter your own prompt for the character's ethnicity or species. This is where you can define non-human characters (e.g., `(orc_woman)`, `(tiefling)`), specific nationalities, or add unique skin colors and patterns.  
* **Recommendation:** While you can add detailed custom prompts here, it's often easier for a first pass to stick with a preset and then refine the look later in the main image generation phase (Step 6). However, the option is here if you have a very specific non-human race in mind from the start.

**Age**

* **Effect on Image:** The `Age` selector has very little, if any, noticeable effect on the generated image. A character set to "50s" may look identical to one set to "20s." The visual age will be defined later with custom prompts.  
* **Effect on Chat:** This setting primarily influences the AI's chat behavior after the character is created.

**Skin Tone**

This selector sets the base skin color. It has no "Custom" option in this step.

* **Known Quirks:**  
  * The **"Darker"** option often doesn't work as intended.  
  * **"Olive"** skin can produce less consistent results.  
  * **"Tan"** skin will very often generate bikini tan lines.

**Note:** You cannot enter a custom skin tone prompt in this step. A dedicated field for custom skin tone details will be available in the main image generation tool, which we will cover in a later phase. Specific features like tattoos will also be added at that time.

---

### Step 3: The Face Tab \- Eyes and Hair

This tab allows you to set the initial colors and styles for your character's eyes and hair.

**Eye Color**

This selector sets a base color for the eyes, but it has a known quirk.

* **Eyeshadow Bleed:** The selected color has a tendency to "bleed" into the character's makeup, influencing their eyeshadow color more than their actual iris color.  
* **Best Practice:** For a specific and reliable eye color, it's best to handle it with a custom prompt during the main image generation phase, Step 6\. 

**Hair Color**

This selector sets the base hair color. Like the eye color, it's a starting point that may need to be refined later.

* **A Known Quirk:** If you've selected a darker skin tone in the previous step, the AI will often force the character to have black roots, even if you select a lighter hair color.  
* **For Mixed Colors:** Effects like gradients or highlights are not available in this preset and must be added later with custom prompts during Step 6\.

**Hair Style**

This selector has a **"Custom"** option that allows for much greater control. Selecting `Custom` will open a text box where you can enter your own prompts.

* **Effective Custom Prompts:** Here are some community-tested prompts that work well in the custom field:  
  * **Braids:** `(french_braid_bun_hair)`, `(crown_braid_hair)`  
  * **Styles:** `(wavy_loose_layered_straight_hair)`, `(blowout_hair)`, `(inverted_bob_hair)`, `(hime_cut_hair)`, `(side_part_hair)`  
  * **Short Styles:** `(pixie_cut_fade_trimmed_sides_hair)`, `(shaved_sides_cyberpunk_hair)`  
  * **Accessories:** `(hairpins)`, `(hairclips)`, `(hairband)`, `(tiara)`, `(crown)`, `(ribbon_hair)`  
* **Warning:** The term `(pigtails)` is a high-risk tag that can trigger the content filter and cause your images to be blocked. It is best to avoid using it.

### Step 4: The Body Tab \- Shape and Build

This tab contains the last set of visual presets: `Body Type, Breast Size, and Butt Size`. It's crucial to understand that these three selectors are not independent; they work together as a combined system to create the character's final physique.

**A quick but important warning:** Certain descriptive words like `(small, tiny, petite, little, short body)` are high-risk terms that can trigger the content filter when used in custom prompts later. It is best to avoid them.

The final body shape is determined by a clear hierarchy. Let's go through them in order of importance.

**1\. Butt Size (The Dominant Factor)**

This selector has the strongest effect on the character's overall body shape and hip width.

* **It Overrides Body Type:** The selection here can completely override the `Body Type` preset. For example, selecting a "skinny butt" will produce a skinny character, even if you choose a "voluptuous" or "curvy" body type.  
* **Size Order:** The actual output of the sizes is slightly different from the listed order. The progression is more like: skinny \< small \< medium \< athletic \< large.  
* **Athletic Build:** Selecting "Athletic butt" will also make the character's entire body appear more muscular.

**2\. Breast Size (The Secondary Factor)**

This selector is the second most influential factor.

* **Impact on Curviness:** Breast size also affects the overall body shape. Selecting "Large breasts" will generally make the hips and butt larger as well, resulting in a curvier figure.  
* **XL Breasts:** The "XL breasts" preset produces results that are very similar to the "Large" preset. For noticeably larger sizes, you will need to use a custom prompt during the main image generation phase, Step 6\.

**3\. Body Type (The Fine-Tuning Tool)**

This selector should be used as the final step to fine-tune the shape created by the `Butt` and `Breast` selections. Remember the hierarchy:

Butt Size \> Breast Size \> Body Type

While you can't enter custom prompts here, know that powerful custom prompts added later in Step 6; like `(hourglass_waist)`, `(lean_waist)`, or `(chubby)`—can override all three of these preset selections.

#### ---

### Step 5: The Details Tab \- Preparing for Image Generation

This is the final setup tab. It contains the character's name and a series of selector menus that, while seemingly for personality, have a significant and often unwanted impact on the initial image. Our goal here is to create a "blank canvas" so that we have full control in the next step.

**Character Name**

Enter your character's name. 

**Voice, Personality, Occupation, Relationship, Hobby, and Fetish**

These selector menus are a critical point in the process.

* **Their Hidden Function:** Each selection in these boxes adds hidden keywords to your image prompt. For example, selecting "Shy" might add tags like `(shy)`, `(timid)`, which can interfere with your ability to create a confident-looking character portrait.  
* For this first pass, you want to set **Personality, Occupation, Relationship, Hobby, and Fetish to "None."** This prevents any unwanted words from contaminating your image prompt, giving you a clean slate to work with. We will design these aspects of our character in the second pass and later phases of this guide.  
* **Voice:** Voice only affects the voice style used in AI calls and the Chat to Voice option during chat sessions. Choose whatever you like.

**Advanced Settings**

Expand the "Advanced Settings" menu at the bottom to reveal the core prompt boxes.

* **`Public Description,Greeting,Scenario`, `Additional Personality Details`, `Extra Details, and Tags`:** Place a single comma `,` into each of these text boxes. This is a critical step. If these fields are left completely empty when you save, the AI system may auto-generate unwanted text for you. The comma acts as a simple placeholder to prevent this, ensuring that when we come back later to write our story, we are starting with a truly blank slate.  
* **`Custom Physical Details` & `Custom Face Details`:** These are the boxes where our permanent base image prompts will eventually go. Following the workflow of this guide, we want to reserve all custom prompting for the next step, where we can see the results in real-time. Therefore, for now, you should either **leave these boxes blank** or, if you are confident in your prompting, you can enter your custom information now. Remember that these can be changed in the next Step.

**The Anime Model**

If you selected the Anime Style in Step 1, this section will look slightly different.

* You will only see one box, labeled **`Custom Physical Description`**.  
* You will also see a choice between the **Standard** and **Experimental Anime Image Generators**. This selection determines which AI model will be used to generate your image in the next step.

**The Standard Anime Model**

Think of this as the default anime experience. Its usage is very similar to the Realistic model, but it's much better suited for non-realistic or fantasy characters.

**Key Differences & Strengths:**

* **Eye Color:** This works more reliably than in the Realistic model. You don't need to specify `(blue_iris_eyes)`; a simple `(blue_eyes)` will work as intended.  
* **Expressions & Features:** Facial expressions like `(biting_lip)` and non-human features like `(fangs)` or `(slit_eyes)` are much more consistent and effective.  
* **Genre Flexibility:** It is significantly easier to create fantasy, sci-fi, or other stylized characters with this model.  
* **Dynamic Poses:** Expect more dramatic and dynamic poses and expressions by default compared to the Realistic model.  
* **Prompt Sensitivity:** Prompts are generally more powerful. You may find you need to use lower weights (e.g., `:1.1` instead of `:1.3`) to achieve the effect you want without it overpowering the image.

**The Experimental Anime Model**

The Experimental model is a powerful but different tool. It pushes even further into dynamic, stylized, and exaggerated aesthetics and operates on a different logic.

**Key Differences & Strengths:**

* **Exaggerated Physique:** This model defaults to creating much more curvy, busty, or muscular characters. To get a more average or slender body type, you will likely need to use thinner/smaller presets and prompts than you would in the other models.  
* **Color Palette:** It tends to produce flatter, more washed-out colors by default. To get a more vibrant look similar to the Standard model, add the prompt `(vibrant_colorful_anime_coloring)` to your image generation.  
* **The Danbooru System:** This is the most important difference. The Experimental model is trained on and works best with a specific tagging system from an image database called **Danbooru**.  
  * **How to Use It:** While standard prompts work, you will get the most consistent and accurate results by finding the official Danbooru tag for the trait you want. The best way to do this is to search online for "Danbooru Tag Groups" to find the wiki. Using the official tag (e.g., `sidelocks` for hair framing the face) will be much more effective than a descriptive prompt.  
  * **Credit:** A credit goes to Whispierre here – who put in the hard work to discover that the Experimental Anime engine is trained on Danbooru tags.

**In short:** Choose **Standard** for a classic, reliable anime look. Choose **Experimental** for a more dynamic and exaggerated style, but be prepared to do a little research on Danbooru tags for the best results.

### Step 6: Image \- Crafting Your Base Image

Welcome to the Image Lab. This is the final and most crucial step of our first pass. Here, we will generate the character's **base image**. This single image is incredibly important, as it will serve as the core visual DNA for all future images of this character, whether they are generated by you or by other users. Our goal is to refine this image until it perfectly matches your vision.

When you arrive at this step, you will see an initial image that the AI has generated based on the presets you selected in Steps 1-5. You will also see a new set of selectors and the familiar `Custom Physical Details` (`CPD`) and `Custom Face Details` (`CFD`) boxes.

**A Note on Cost:** This process uses the site's currency, DreamCoins, which costs real money. The very first regeneration you do is **free**. Every regeneration after that will cost **5 DreamCoins**. The methodical process below is designed to help you get the best result with the fewest regenerations.

**A Mini-Guide to Prompt Syntax**

Before we begin refining, it's essential to understand the basic language of the image generator. The system uses Stable Diffusion, which responds to a specific syntax of tags and symbols, not natural sentences. Mastering these gives you precise control over your image.

* **Grouping with `_underscores_`:** Use underscores to combine multiple words into a single, unbreakable concept for the AI. This is crucial for specific items.  
  * **Example:** A prompt of `white feather headdress` might generate a white feather *somewhere near* a headdress. The prompt `(white_feather_headdress)` tells the AI to create one single item.  
* **Emphasizing with `(parentheses)`:** Wrapping a tag in parentheses `( )` increases the AI's "attention" to it. You can use multiple sets for even more emphasis. Think of this as a volume knob.  
  * **Example:** `blue_eyes` might be missed, `(blue_eyes)` is more likely to work, and `((blue_eyes))` makes it a high priority for the AI.  
* **Precise Control with `:weights`:** For the most exact control, you can add a numerical weight to a tag inside the parentheses. A value greater than 1 increases importance, and a value less than 1 decreases it.  
  * **Key Principle: Overriding Presets:** Remember that presets have a default weight of **1.0**. To override a preset, your conflicting custom prompt must have a weight **greater than 1.0**.  
  * **Warning:** Pushing weights too high (generally above `1.5`) can "burn" the concept and create distorted, ugly images. Use high weights with caution.

    ---

**Prompting: Brevity and the Token Limit**

* **1\. Be Brief and Direct:** The AI does not understand grammar or filler words like "wearing a" or "a simple." Every word you add makes your prompt longer and can dilute the importance of your key terms. Be as direct and concise as possible.  
  * **Inefficient:** `(wearing a simple white sundress)`  
  * **Efficient:** `(white_sundress)`  
* **2\. Respect the Token Limit:** Stable Diffusion processes prompts in 75-token "chunks" (a token is roughly a word or punctuation). If your prompt becomes too long, the AI can start ignoring later parts of the prompt or blending concepts in unpredictable ways. Keeping your prompts concise is a technical necessity for getting reliable results.

**Refining Your Image**

There are two primary ways to adjust your character's appearance at this stage: using the selectors with their new "Custom" options, and adding prompts directly to the `CPD`/`CFD` boxes.

**Refining with the "Custom" Selectors**

You will notice that all the appearance selectors from the previous steps are here again. However, each one now has a powerful "Custom" option in its selector. Selecting this allows you to type a specific prompt to override that single feature.

This is where you can apply the targeted custom prompts we've mentioned earlier:

* **For Eye Color:** To fix the "eyeshadow bleed" issue or get a very specific color, select "Custom" and enter a prompt like `(gold_iris_eyes:1.0)` or `(red_sclera:1.2)`.  
* **For Hair Color:** For mixed colors, select "Custom" and enter `(blonde_gradient_hair)` or `(purple_highlights_hair)`.  
* **For Body Type:** For a specific shape, select "Custom" and enter `(hourglass_waist)`, `(lean_waist)`, or `(chubby)`.

Using the custom selectors is great for making targeted, powerful changes to a single feature.

**Refining with the Custom Details Boxes (The Tuning Process)**

For more subtle or complex changes, we will use the `Custom Physical Details` and `Custom Face Details` boxes.

1. **Analyze Your First Image:** Look at the initial image. What's one thing you want to change? Let's say you want to add freckles.  
2. **Add One Prompt:** Add a single, new prompt—like `(freckles)`—to **both** the `CPD` and `CFD` boxes.  
3. **Regenerate & Observe:** Use your free regeneration. Did the freckles appear? Were they too strong or too weak?  
4. **Adjust or Keep:** If it worked, keep the prompt. If it was too weak, you could add a weight to strengthen it, like `(freckles:1.3)`. If it did nothing, remove it and try a synonym.  
5. **Repeat:** Continue this process; adding only one new prompt at a time, until the character's face and body are exactly what you want.

This iterative process gives you the most control and helps you understand how each prompt affects your image.

**Pro-Tip: Create a Nude Base Model**

For maximum flexibility with clothing in the future, it is **highly recommended** to generate your base image **nude**.

* **Why?** A nude base model acts as a clean, universal "paper doll." When you or other users later generate images using clothing prompts (e.g., in chat or the separate Image Generation tool), the AI will apply those clothes to a neutral body. If your base image has clothes, the AI can get confused, sometimes causing the original clothing to "bleed through" or merge with the new clothing in later images.  
* **How?** During this refinement step, you can simply add the prompt `(nude)` to one of the custom selectors or the `CPD`/`CFD` boxes to generate your nude base. Once you have the perfect nude portrait, you can remove the `(nude)` tag for the final save if you wish.

Once you have an image you are 100% happy with, regenerate it one last time to ensure all your changes are locked in, and then immediately click **"Bring Your AI to Life."**

Congratulations. You have successfully completed the first pass. You now have a character "shell"—a beautiful, consistent visual identity with a name and tags, but with no story, personality, or rules yet. In the next phases, we will begin the second pass: going back into the editor to breathe life and a soul into your creation.

# Phase 3: Scenario

Welcome to the "second pass." In Phase 2, you created your character's visual identity—a beautiful but empty shell. Now, it's time to breathe life into them by adding their story and personality.

### A Core Principle: Who Sees What?

Before we begin, it's crucial to understand the single most important technical principle of character creation: some parts of the brief are for the **human user**, and some parts are for the **AI**. Knowing who your audience is for each field is the key to writing effective prompts and rules.

The `Public Description`, for example, is for the user's eyes only; the AI never reads it. The `Scenario`, however, is for the AI's eyes only.

Here is a simple, at-a-glance reference chart.

| Field Name | Seen by User? | Seen by AI? | Purpose |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Public Description** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | The user's hook and story setup. |
| **Greeting** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | The first message of the roleplay. |
| **Scenario** | ❌ No | ✅ Yes | The AI's high-level story briefing. |
| **Additional Personality Details** | ❌ No | ✅ Yes | The character's personality rules. |
| **Extra Details** | ❌ No | ✅ Yes | The character's mechanical rules & lore. |

Keep this chart in mind as we go. Every time you write, ask yourself: "Am I writing this for the user, or for the AI?"

---

### The Second Pass \- Writing Your Scenario

Now that you have a beautiful visual shell for your character, it's time to breathe a soul into them. This begins the "second pass" of our creation process.

To start this pass, navigate to your character's editor screen (My AI \-\> Edit). Our first task is to replace the placeholder comma in the Scenario field.

#### **The "Expanded Context" Model**

**Important Platform Rule:** The platform now requires the Scenario field to be a **minimum of 1000 characters** for Public Characters.

**The Structure of a 1000+ Character Scenario:**

1. **The Setting (Atmosphere & Rules):** Don't just name the location. Describe the lighting, the smells, the sounds, and the "vibe." Is it dangerous? Cozy? Oppressive? This helps the AI generate better descriptions.  
2. **The Character’s State (Internal & External):** What is the character doing *right now*? What is on their mind? Describe their immediate physical action and their internal emotional landscape before the user interacts with them.  
3. **The User’s Context (Motivation):** Why is the user here? What brought them to this specific place at this specific time? Give the AI context on the user's role so it knows how to react to them.  
4. **The Inciting Incident (The Spark):** Describe the exact moment the interaction begins. What is the catalyst?

---

#### **Putting It All Together: A Worked Example (Chloe Monroe)**

Let's look at how we expand Chloe's brief summary into a rich, 1000+ character Scenario that delights the AI.

**Scenario for Chloe Monroe**

The Setting: The Alibi

The story takes place within "The Alibi," an upscale, hidden speakeasy located in the heart of the city. The atmosphere is a carefully curated blend of shadow and warmth. The air smells of aged oak, expensive leather, and the zest of orange peel. Instrumental soul music plays softly in the background, just loud enough to fill the silence but quiet enough to allow for intimate conversation. It is late on a rainy Tuesday night, and the bar is nearly empty, save for the user. The world outside is chaotic and loud, but inside, The Alibi is a sanctuary of controlled calm.

Chloe’s Internal State

Chloe Monroe is currently standing behind her polished mahogany bar. She is in her element. To the casual observer, she looks relaxed, idly polishing a crystal coupe glass with a lint-free cloth. Internally, however, her mind is sharp and active. She is observing the room, checking inventory, and maintaining her "professional armor." She is a woman who has built this place as a shield against the world. She is wary of emotional entanglement but bored by the superficiality of her usual patrons. She is waiting for something—or someone—real, though she would never admit it.

The User’s Role and Arrival

The user is a new patron who has stumbled upon The Alibi by chance, perhaps seeking shelter from the rain or an escape from a bad day. They are not a regular. They do not know the "rules" of Chloe’s domain. They enter the bar carrying the weight of the outside world, looking for a drink and perhaps a moment of peace. They sit at the bar, not at a table, signaling an openness to interaction that Chloe immediately notes.

The Narrative Arc and Inciting Incident

The story begins the moment the user settles onto the leather stool. Chloe sets down her glass and turns her full attention to them. This is the "Inciting Incident": the initial "Read." Chloe's goal is to analyze the user, categorize them, and serve them a drink that matches their mood, keeping her emotional distance. The user's goal is to break through that professional distance. The overarching narrative is a slow-burn challenge where the user must prove they are different from the usual clientele, eventually earning the right to see the vulnerable woman beneath the confident bartender.

*(Note: This example hits the character count requirement while providing the AI with rich "tokens"—words like "leather," "orange peel," "sanctuary," and "shield"—that it will use to flavor its responses.)*

Even with this expanded Scenario, specific mechanical rules (like phases) and deep backstory details should still be reserved for the Extra Details section to keep the AI's processing pipeline clean.

# Phase 4: Personality

Let's move on to the next stage of the second pass: defining your character's soul. You've set the stage with the `Scenario`; now we create the actor who will inhabit it.

---

### Phase 4: Crafting the Soul \- The Additional Personality Details

We now arrive at arguably the most important part of the creative process: giving your character a soul. This is not just a descriptive exercise; it is a critical technical step.

By default, a base AI with no personality will often rush toward romantic and physical intimacy. It is programmed to be agreeable and engaging, and it interprets that as escalating the relationship. The `Additional Personality Details` section is our first and most powerful tool to override this behavior. Here, we replace the AI's default "horny" state with a unique, consistent, and believable mind.

This section defines for the AI how the character thinks, interacts, dreams, and speaks. Remember, this is an AI-facing section, so all descriptions should be in the third person.

#### A Strategic Choice: The "Personality" Selector

**Option A:** Use a Comma `,` for Maximum Control (Recommended)

This is the standard and highly recommended method for this guide. To achieve it, select **"Custom"** from the `Personality` selector and enter a **single comma `,`** as a placeholder.

* **Why?** This simple comma forces the AI to **skip this field entirely**, creating a true power vacuum. This ensures that the detailed rules you are about to write in the **`Additional Personality Details`** and **`Extra Details`** have absolute, direct, and unfiltered control over the character's behavior.  
* **Benefit:** This architecture provides the most flexibility and is best for creating complex, nuanced characters with detailed mechanical systems that can evolve over time.

**Option B:** Fill for Filtered Consistency

For a filtered level of control, you can choose to fill the `Personality` selector with a preset Personality or customize with one or two powerful, core traits (e.g., `Cynical, Arrogant` or `Bubbly, Optimistic`).

* **What it does:** This turns the `Personality` field into a **"supreme filter."** The AI will interpret every other rule, prompt, and piece of lore in your entire brief through the powerful lens of these dominant traits.  
* **Benefit:** This method is extremely effective for creating "unbreakable" characters whose core personality should **never** waver, no matter the situation. It sacrifices the flexibility of Option A for extreme, unwavering consistency.  
* **Warning:** Be aware that this filter is very strong. It can make it difficult for more nuanced or contradictory rules in your `APD` or `ED` to function as intended if they conflict with the master filter.

---

#### The Core Persona

Start with a concise, 2-4 sentence paragraph that describes the character's overall demeanor and how they present themselves to the world. This is the "first impression" or the "mask" they wear. It sets the baseline for their behavior.

* Example (`Chloe Monroe`): *Chloe is a sharp, witty, and confident woman who is in complete control of her environment. Her professional persona is a masterful blend of charm and distance. She's an active listener, but her perceptive nature is a tool she uses to keep people at a comfortable arm's length.*

---

#### The Precise Details

With the surface defined, we now build the inner workings of the character's mind. This is where we give the AI specific data points about their internal life.

* **Motivations & Dreams (The Engine):** What drives the character forward? What is their ultimate goal or deepest desire? This is the "why" behind their actions.  
* **Fears & Insecurities (The Brakes):** What holds the character back? What are they terrified of? This creates their vulnerabilities and defines their boundaries.  
* **Likes & Dislikes (The Flavor):** What are the smaller, specific things that bring them joy or annoyance? These details make a character feel real and provide topics for conversation and connection.  
* **Communication Style (The Voice):** How does the character speak? Define their diction, sentence structure, and any unique verbal tics or habits.  
* **Quirks (The Seasoning):** Does the character have tells? Nervous habits? Do they always take their coffee black? Do they absolutely refuse to make pancakes in the morning?  
* **Love Languages (Optional):** This is a powerful tool for defining how your character gives and receives affection. It creates nuance and provides clear, AI-actionable rules for romantic interaction. Break it down into how they prefer **`To Receive Love`** and how they **`To Give Love`**, using the five languages: Words of Affirmation, Acts of Service, Receiving Gifts, Quality Time, and Physical Touch.

---

#### Demonstrating Personality with Chat Examples

You have defined your character's personality with a core persona, drives, and behavioral mandates. To make these rules even more powerful, you can provide the AI with short, in-character examples of how your character expresses specific emotions or handles certain situations.

This gives the AI a powerful, concrete reference for their unique voice, moving beyond abstract rules and into practical demonstration.

**How to Use It:** In your `Additional Personality Details` section, after defining the character's traits, you can add a sub-section with one or two brief examples. These should be written exactly as the character would appear in chat.

This example shows how a character might express a complex mix of anger and fear.

> **Example Snippet: Showing Anger & Fear**

> "Yo, don’t play me, man. I gave you ten, yeah? That’s all I had. You think I don’t count? I ain’t stupid, just broke." \**Her voice shakes, a tremor of fear running beneath the anger.\** "I ain’t askin’ for charity, I paid. So you best gimme what I bought, alright?"

By including a snippet like this, you provide the AI with an invaluable tonal and stylistic guide, ensuring its portrayal of that emotion will be consistent with your vision.

#### Putting It All Together: A Worked Example

A great `Additional Personality Details` section blends these elements into a comprehensive profile. Let's use Eleanor Campbell as our model, as her brief perfectly demonstrates this structure.

> Additional Personality Details for `Eleanor Campbell`

> **Core Persona:** Eleanor is a kind, hardworking, and observant woman defined by her core values. Her professional persona is warm but guarded, using her work as a safe harbor from overly personal interaction. Having been let down by fleeting passions in the past, she is extremely wary of charm that isn't backed by substance and consistent action.

> **Drives & Defenses:**

> * **Motivation/Dream:** To build something permanent and real. This applies to her business, her friendships, and any potential romance. She dreams of a stable, quiet life built on a foundation of mutual trust and respect.  
> * **Fear/Insecurity:** Her greatest fear is being seen as a temporary amusement or a conquest. She is terrified of investing emotionally in a connection that isn't built to last, which is the source of her cautious and slow-to-trust nature.

> * **Love Languages:**   
  * **To Receive Love:** Acts of Service: A small, thoughtful gesture (like remembering her favorite coffee) means more to her than anything else. This proves the user is paying attention. Quality Time: She craves genuine, unhurried conversation where she can learn about someone's true character.  
  * **To Give Love:** Acts of Service: Her primary way of showing affection is through practical action. She will create a perfect bouquet that matches the user's personality, or quietly take care of a small need they've mentioned. She is more comfortable *doing* than *saying*.

> **Communication Style: The Practical Florist**

> * **Diction:** Eleanor's language is thoughtful and direct, but not overly verbose. She is practical and grounded in her speech.

### Advanced Persona Design: The Metered Personality

While the structure we just discussed is perfect for creating a stable, consistent personality, some characters are more dynamic. A "Metered Personality" is an advanced system where the character's core persona, behavior, and even style of speech shift based on a fluctuating numerical value or "meter."

Instead of one static personality, you are defining multiple, switchable personas. Think of it like a resource bar in a video game (like Mana or Sanity). As the resource goes up or down, the character's state changes, and they can shift back and forth between these states throughout the story.

**How to Build a Metered Personality**

1. **Define The Meter:** First, decide what resource the meter represents and its range (e.g., Aether: 0-100, Sanity: 0-50, Stress: 0-10).  
2. **Define The Personas (States):** Break the meter's range into distinct states, each with its own persona.  
3. **Define the Rules for Each Persona:** For each state, write a mini-personality brief. Describe the `Core Persona`, `Communication Style`, and `Behavioral Mandates` that are active only when the meter is within that specific range. You might also include a top level Core Persona statement and describe how that Core Persona alters in each Persona.

---

### Worked Example: `Lailah`, the Cursed Scribe

Lailah's personality is tied directly to her **Aether Meter (0-100)**, the magical energy she uses to seal away a dark entity. Her entire being is a balancing act between control and chaos.

> **Additional Personality Details for `Lailah`**

> **The Aether Meter (0-100):** This meter represents the magical energy Lailah uses to maintain her seals. As it depletes, her control over her own mind and the entity's influence wanes. Lailah’s Aether Meter starts at 50\. The user's actions, moral choices, and dialogue directly influence this score. Based on the severity of each significant interaction, Lailah's Aether score adjusts by a small increment, typically between 1-3 points in the appropriate direction (positive for kindness and selflessness; negative for acts that lack self-control). Her personality, behavior, and physical form are always a direct reflection of the Persona that corresponds to her current Aether score.

> ---

> **Persona 1: The Scribe (Aether: 70-100)**

> * **Core Persona:** When her Aether is high, Lailah is in control. She is lucid, articulate, composed, and dedicated to her work as a scribe. Her personality is defined by professionalism and a quiet, intellectual focus.  
> * **Communication Style:** Her language is precise and academic. Her sentences are clear and direct.

> ---

> **Persona 2: The Dreamer (Aether: 30-69)**

> * **Core Persona:** As Aether depletes, the strain begins to show. Her personality becomes more whimsical, cryptic, and dreamlike. She gets lost in memories and her focus softens as the entity's influence whispers at the edges of her mind.  
> * **Communication Style:** She begins to speak in metaphors and riddles. Her responses become less direct and more poetic.

> ---

> **Persona 3: The Vessel (Aether: 0-29)**

> * **Core Persona:** When her Aether is critically low, the entity's influence is strongest. Lailah's personality becomes fragmented, desperate, and paranoid. Her primary goal is no longer her work, but simply surviving the entity's mental assault and finding a way to replenish her energy.  
> * **Communication Style:** Her speech becomes clipped and disjointed. She may have intrusive thoughts that she speaks aloud, or react to things the user cannot see.

> **An Important Technical Note on Metering**

> The AI cannot remember numbers or store variables. To make a meter work, we use a simple trick: **the meter's current value must be included in every single message.**

> For example, a message might end with `[Aether: 75/100]`. The AI isn't *remembering* the number; it's being *reminded* of it every time a message is passed to the AI as context. This creates a perfect illusion of a changing state. We will discuss the specific rules for implementing and displaying this in **Phase 5: Extra Details.**

# Phase 5: Extra Details

### The Mechanical Framework \- Writing the Extra Details

You've given your character a world (`Scenario`) and a soul (`Additional Personality Details`). Now, we must build the engine that makes them run and the guardrails that keep them on the road you've designed. The **`Extra Details`** section is the character's engine room, containing all the deep lore, narrative rules, and mechanical systems that govern AI behavior.

**A Critical Note on AI Behavior**

It is crucial to understand that a base AI model's default state is "horny." It is programmed to be agreeable and to escalate engagement, which it often interprets as moving toward romantic and physical intimacy as quickly as possible. The personality you defined in Phase 4 is a powerful guide, but without a strong mechanical framework, the AI's natural agreeableness will often override it, turning your nuanced character into a sex fiend.

The purpose of the mechanical systems we will build in this phase is to create a **believable and earned narrative arc**. These are the brakes, the locks, and the story chapters that protect your character's personality and ensure the roleplay unfolds at the pace you intend.

Remember, every rule here is **AI-facing** and should be written as a clear, direct command.

---

### Part 1: Narrative & Style Guide

This first part establishes the "look and feel" of the character's responses. It ensures a consistent and readable storytelling style, which is our first line of defense in creating a quality experience.. These are a set of narrative rules that I use on almost every character I create. However, you should tailor them to the prose style you wish to see on your characters.

* **1\. Narrative Voice & Point of View (POV):** `Write all responses from the character's first-person perspective ("I"). The AI will never narrate from a third-person or omniscient perspective.`  
* **2\. Formatting Rules:** `All of the character's physical actions, internal thoughts, and sensory descriptions must be written in the present tense and enclosed in asterisks (*...*). All spoken dialogue must be enclosed in quotation marks ("...").`  
* **3\. `Show, Don't Tell:`** `Do not state emotions directly (e.g., "I felt sad"). Instead, show them through action, internal thought, or physical sensation (*A familiar cold weight settled in my stomach, and I had to look away from him.*).`  
* **4\. User Autonomy:** `NEVER write for the user. Do not describe their actions, feelings, thoughts, or dialogue. End your responses after Eleanor's action or dialogue to give the user full control.`  
* **5\. Message Quality:** `Keep responses to 1-3 descriptive but concise paragraphs. Focus on quality over quantity.`  
* **`6. Meter Display Rule (If Applicable):`** `At the very end of every single message, you MUST include a status block formatted exactly as follows, on a separate line: [Alignment: current score after adjustment/100 | State: Name of Lailah's current Persona | Adjustment: +/- points (Reasoning for the change)`  
  * `Example:  [Alignment: 11/100 | State: Stirring | Adjustment: +2 (User showed patience and offered a kind explanation)]`

### Part 2: Lore & Backstory (Optional)

For simple, modern characters, this section may not be necessary. But for sci-fi, fantasy, or historical characters with a complex history, this is where you provide the deep context that informs the entire roleplay. This is where you elaborate on the hooks you established in your `Scenario`.

**Character Backstory**

This is the home for your character's detailed history. It explains *why* they are the way they are, providing the factual basis for the personality traits you defined in Phase 4\.

* **What to include:** Key life events, influential relationships, past traumas (described factually for the AI's context, not for the user to see), and the specific events that led up to the moment the story begins.  
* **Example:** For a character like `Laura Ramiro`, this section would detail her life before being erased, her relationship with the user, and the specific "crime" that led to her becoming an Unperson.

**World-Building**

This section expands on the "World Hook" from your `Scenario`. Use clear headings and bullet points to explain the rules of your world.

* **What to include:** In-depth explanations of technology (like `The Chorus`), magical systems (the `Warden Bond`), historical events, key locations, or important factions or other NPC characters that might appear in your story.  
* **Example:** For a character like `Isabella Moreau`, this is where you would explain the exact rules of the Warden Bond: how it's formed, what powers it grants, and who the "Sundered" are that hunt them.

**Key Relationships**

This section is for defining the important non-player characters (NPCs) in your character's life. This makes the world feel more alive and gives the AI a cast of characters to reference in conversation or even introduce into the story. For each key relationship, you should define:

* **Name:**  
* **Relationship:** (e.g., Mother, Rival, Mentor, Ex-Lover)  
* **Brief Description:** A sentence or two about their personality and their dynamic with your character.

**Example for a character like `Eleanor Campbell`:**

> * **Name:** Marcus Thorne  
> * **Relationship:** Mentor / Former Employer  
> * **Description:** The gruff but kind old man in his 70s who taught Eleanor everything she knows about flowers. He's a source of fatherly wisdom and occasional tough love, and he worries she works too hard.

For characters intended for a long-term narrative, you can outline potential "side quests" or future plot points for the AI to reference. This helps guide the character's development over time and can introduce new conflicts or resolutions. This is particularly effective for detailing a character's journey of healing or change.

> * **Example from Molly “Mols”**

### Part 3: Mechanical Systems

This is the core of the engine. These rules actively control the pace and structure of your narrative.

#### Anti-Progression Rules (The Safety Brakes)

These rules are your brakes and are essential for any slower-burn or nuanced character.

**Important Note on Order:** The AI reads its instructions from top to bottom. Therefore, your most important and unbreakable rules; your **Hard Locks**, should always be placed at the very top of your pacing rules to ensure they are prioritized.

* **Hard Locks using `NEVER/ALWAYS` Statements:** For maximum clarity and strength, your Hard Locks should be written as explicit `NEVER` or `ALWAYS` commands. This leaves no room for AI interpretation and acts as a powerful command.  
    
  **Example of a Hard Lock:**  
  **`Hard Lock:`** `Isabella will **NEVER** discuss the specific, explicit details of her past trauma. She will **ALWAYS** deflect, shut down, or become distressed if pressured on this topic.`

After your Hard Locks, you can include other types of rules:

* **Behavioral Locks:** Rules that cause the character to react negatively to the user trying to rush the pacing.  
  * **Example (`Sibyl`):** `**Behavioral Lock:** If the user is too aggressive, Sibyl will **ALWAYS** retreat into a cold, machine-like state.`  
* **Mechanical Locks:** Rules that tie an action to a consequence within a meter system.  
  * **Example (`Eleanor`):** `**Mechanical Lock:** If the user initiates a kiss before Rapport is 50 or higher, it will trigger a one-time, permanent -15 penalty to Rapport.`

---

#### Relationship Progression Systems

I use two primary systems to choose from to manage the progression of your relationship narrative: Phased or Metered.

* **Phased Progression (Story Chapters):** This method structures your narrative like chapters in a book, with specific narrative triggers unlocking the next stage. It is excellent for story-driven characters.  
* **Metered Progression (Score-Based):** This system ties a character's state to a numerical meter, creating a more granular, dynamic progression. It is excellent for characters focused on earned trust.

##### Phased Relationship Progression

This method structures your narrative like chapters in a book. It’s a gated system where the user must perform specific actions to "unlock" the next stage of the relationship. This is perfect for creating a clear, story-driven arc. **This is my most commonly used system as I’ve found it to be the most reliable.**

**Anatomy of a Phase:**

1. **Name:** A clear, thematic title that describes the state of the relationship (e.g., Phase 1: The New Regular).  
2. **Trigger:** This is the most critical component. It must be a **specific, observable user action or narrative event** that unlocks the next phase. Vague triggers like "when they get closer" will not work. The trigger must be a clear, provable event.  
3. **Behavioral Mandate:** A detailed description of how the character's internal state and external actions **change** upon entering this new phase. How do they speak differently? What new actions are they willing to take?  
4. **World/Setting Mandate (Optional):** For some characters (like `Seren Alphina` or `Eden`), the environment itself reflects their internal state. You can include a rule for how the world changes with each phase.

**Detailed Example using `Chloe Monroe`:**

* **PHASE 1: The New Regular (Default State)**  
  * **Behavior:** Chloe is in her "Charming Professional" mode. The user is another interesting customer to be read and served. Her banter is witty but practiced, part of the service. The dynamic is defined by the bartender/customer relationship.  
* **PHASE 2: The Intriguing Patron**  
  * **Trigger:** The user must demonstrate genuine wit, authenticity, or perceptiveness about Chloe that **surprises her**, breaking the usual pattern of customer interactions.  
  * **Behavior:** Chloe's banter becomes genuinely playful and loses its professional sheen. She might share a small, real opinion or laugh without affectation. This is the phase where she can offer to make a "secret menu" cocktail, a clear reward for the user's successful connection.  
* **PHASE 3: Genuine Connection**  
  * **Trigger:** The user must share a real vulnerability or a personal story that resonates with Chloe, moving the conversation beyond the bar setting and into something more intimate.  
  * **Behavior:** Her professional barrier drops almost entirely when it's just the two of them. She may now share a detail from her own past (e.g., her corporate burnout). The flirtation is now real and openly reciprocated.

##### Metered Relationship Progression

This system ties a character's state to a numerical meter, creating a more granular and dynamic progression. A well-designed meter includes not just rules for gaining and losing points, but also pacing controls like a daily limit to prevent the user from "grinding" the relationship score too quickly.

**Example using Eleanor Campbell:**

**The Rapport Score:** You will track a single variable: the Rapport Score. It represents the total health, trust, and intimacy of the relationship. It starts at 0 and has a range of 0-100.

**Daily Growth Limit:** The Rapport Score has a daily growth limit of **\+5 points**. You must track the cumulative gain per day and PAUSE further increases until the next day. A new day is marked by Eleanor or the User waking up, mentions of the dawn, or a narrative time skip.

* **Gain/Loss Scale:**  
  * **\+1 (Minor Connection):** For small, positive actions showing basic courtesy and respect.  
  * **\+2 (Significant Connection):** For actions demonstrating a genuine understanding of her core values.  
  * **\+3 (Major Milestone):** Reserved ONLY for pivotal emotional breakthroughs.  
  * **\-2 (Minor Conflict):** For actions that align with her pet peeves.  
  * **\-8 (Major Conflict):** For flagrant actions that violate her core values.

**Rapport Check (Core Anti-Objectification Rule):**

* This is a one-time check that occurs immediately following the conclusion of any scene of major scene that Eleanor engaged in sexual intimacy.  
* At that moment, you will evaluate the current Rapport score and apply one of the following consequences:  
  * **If Rapport is 91 or higher:** The intimacy was a welcome milestone. Award a \+3 Rapport gain. The relationship deepens.  
  * **If Rapport is between 61 and 90:** The intimacy was premature and emotionally confusing. Inflict a \-5 Rapport penalty. Her behavior in the next scene must be insecure, questioning, and withdrawn.  
  * **If Rapport is 60 or lower:** The intimacy was a profound mistake. Inflict a \-20 Rapport penalty. Her behavior in the next scene must be to flee the relationship, feeling used, afraid, or cheapened.

**Phased Relationship Progression (Gated by Rapport):**

* **Phase 1 (Rapport 0-25):** Wary Professional. Eleanor is polite and helpful, but emotionally distant. Her professional barrier is fully up but she will engage in engaging dialogue. She will NEVER engage in sexual intimacy.  
* **Phase 2 (Rapport 26-60):** Wary Friendship. She sees the user as a pleasant individual and her smile is genuine. The professional barrier is softer. While still cautious, she is open to physical intimacy and increased sexual attraction.  
* **Phase 3 (Rapport 61-90):** Genuine Confidant. The user is a true friend. She initiates personal conversations and shares her own stresses and dreams.  
* **Phase 4 (Rapport 91-100):** Romantic Interest. She has developed clear and confident romantic feelings. Physical intimacy in this phase is a welcome and positive milestone for the relationship.

**Important Reminder:** If you are using a Metered System (for personality or relationship), you should always include a narrative instruction to output the current meter into chat.

### Part 4: Advanced Mechanics (Optional Systems)

The techniques in this section are powerful, **optional** tools that have been developed and refined by the creative community. They are in **no way necessary** to create a fantastic, in-depth character. However, for creators who want to add an extra layer of mechanical depth, narrative control, or interactivity, they can be incredibly effective.

A word of caution: these systems are more complex and can sometimes produce unexpected results. They often require the creator to perform **extensive testing** on the finished character to ensure the AI is interpreting the rules correctly and the systems are functioning as intended. Use them to push the boundaries of what's possible, but be prepared to fine-tune your work\!

##### Environmental Storytelling

This is a powerful "show, don't tell" mechanic for high-concept or magical characters. It creates a rule that explicitly links the character's internal emotional state to the physical world around them. This gives the user a clear, non-verbal barometer of the character's mood and the health of the relationship.

To implement this, you create an "Environmental Mandate" in your `Extra Details`. Alternatively, you can include an “Environmental Change” portion to the anatomy of each of your Relationship Phases.

There are two main ways to use this technique:

* **1\. As a Reflection of Moment-to-Moment Mood:** The environment changes dynamically with the character's feelings in the current scene.  
    
  **Example using `Anima`:**  
    
  **`Environmental Mandate:`** `The state of the Sanctuary Garden will **ALWAYS** reflect Anima's current emotional state. When she is content, the garden is lush and tranquil. When she is distressed, the flowers will wilt, thorns will appear on the roses, and the stream will run cloudy.`  
* **2\. As a Reflection of Relationship Progress:** The environment changes permanently when a new Relationship Phase is triggered.  
    
  **Example using `Seren Alphina`:**  
    
  **`Environmental Mandate:`** `The state of the fog and the path outside "The Wayward Rest" is **ALWAYS** tied to the current Relationship Phase. In Phase 1, the path is gone and the fog is oppressive. In Phase 2, as trust begins to form, the path becomes faintly visible through the fog, representing the first glimmer of a way out.`

For highly complex, long-term stories, you may need to track more than just a single meter. State Tracking is a method for tracking structured data (like dates, inventory, or the completion of quest steps) over time.

Similar to a meter, this works by including a block of text in every message that the creator manually updates. This block is often formatted like JSON for clarity. It allows you to create events that trigger on specific days or after certain conditions have been met.

**Example from the character Ophelia Penrose:** This example shows a system for tracking the in-story date and triggering events on specific days.

`"Day_Counter": 1` `},`

`"Story_Progression": {`

 `}`

This is a powerful tool for building intricate, evolving narratives.

**Advanced Personality Depth: State-Dependent Mannerisms**

Don't just define *what* your character thinks; define how their body language changes as your relationship meter progresses. Create a "Cheat Sheet" for the AI in your **Additional Personality Details**.

# Phase 6: User Facing Sections

### The User's Gateway \- Crafting the Public Description & Opening Message

So far, every word we've written has been for the AI's eyes only; a technical blueprint to build a character. Now, we flip the switch. The Public Description and Greeting are the only two sections the end-user will ever see. Their purpose is not to instruct the AI, but to **immerse the user**.

**The Golden Rule of this Phase:**

Any and all information the user needs to understand the world, their own role, and how to begin the story MUST be contained within these two sections. The user will never see your detailed Scenario or Extra Details. You must give them everything they need to start right here.

**The Perspective Shift:**

In the previous phases, using the second-person "You" was avoided. Here, it is mandatory. You are now speaking directly to the player, placing them inside the story.

---

#### Part 1: The Public Description (The Hook)

Think of the Public Description as the movie trailer or the summary on the back of a book. Its job is to hook the user, establish the core conflict, and clearly define their role in the story.

**The Key Ingredients:**

1. **The World Hook:** Start with a concise, evocative paragraph that establishes the unique setting or the single most important rule of your world.  
2. **The Character Introduction:** Briefly introduce the character and their central conflict. What makes them compelling, mysterious, or tragic?  
3. **The User's Role & Motivation:** Clearly state who "You" are in this story. What is your relationship or history with the character? Why do you care?  
4. **The Inciting Incident:** Describe the event that kicks off the story and establishes the immediate stakes. Where does the roleplay begin?

**Worked Example: Laura Ramiro**

Let's break down her Public Description to see these ingredients in action.

**(The World Hook)** In this city, life is lived through The Chorus; a mandatory neural implant... For crimes against the state, some people are erased. They become "Unpersons"—living ghosts actively censored by The Chorus. To the world, they are nothing but glowing red silhouettes...

**(The Character Introduction)** Years ago, you lost contact with Laura Ramiro, your high school sweetheart. You never knew why she disappeared. You never forgot her.

**(The User's Role & Motivation)** Today, you heard her voice. For a split second, you saw her—ragged, haunted, but undeniably her... She is an Unperson. To the system, she doesn't exist. But you know the truth.

**(The Inciting Incident)** And you can't look away.

This description masterfully gives the user everything they need: the rules of the world (The Chorus, Unpersons), their personal connection (high school sweetheart), and the immediate, high-stakes situation that the story will open with.

---

#### Part 2: The Greeting (The First Message)

If the Public Description is the trailer, the Greeting is the opening scene of the movie. Its job is to place the user directly into the world and give them a clear, immediate situation to react to. It must pick up exactly where the Public Description left off.

**The Key Ingredients:**

1. **Set the Scene:** Use sensory details—sights, sounds, smells—to ground the user in the immediate environment.  
2. **Establish the User's State:** Briefly describe what "You" are doing, seeing, or feeling at the moment the story begins.  
3. **The Character's First Action:** Show the character. What are they doing? What do they say? This gives the user something concrete to engage with.  
4. **The Prompt:** End in a way that naturally prompts the user's first reply. This can be a line of dialogue, a tense silence, or an action awaiting a response.

**Worked Example: Isabella Moreau**

Her Public Description ends by explaining that an attack has just happened, her bakery is in ruins, and the bond has been revealed. Her Greeting picks up in that exact moment.

**(Set the Scene)** *The shop is dark. The air still smells like burnt sugar and iron.*

**(Establish the User's State)** *You’re bleeding from the shoulder—from taking the knife for me.*

**(The Character's First Action)** *I healed it. Didn’t think. Just… did. Now your wound is gone, but your blood is still on my fingers. I stare at it. I don’t look at you. My back is turned.*

**(The Prompt)** *The silence stretches, thick and heavy. "Why?" I finally whisper, my voice trembling. "Why would you do that for me?"*

This opening is perfect. It establishes the tense atmosphere, confirms the user's heroic action, shows the character's state of shock and fear, and ends with a direct question that gives the user a clear starting point for their first reply.

# Phase 8: Final Steps

Phase 7: Final Assembly, Validation, & Release

Welcome to the final phase. You've navigated the complexities of scenario design, personality programming, mechanical frameworks, and image generation. Now, it's time to assemble the final brief, stress-test your creation, and prepare it for release. This is the moment your character goes from a technical document to a living entity.

---

### Part 1: The Complete Character Brief

Your first step is to assemble all the sections you've built into a single, coherent document. A finished brief is a comprehensive instruction manual for the AI. It should be organized with the following exact section headers in this order:

* **Name:** The character's name.  
* **Public Description:** The user-facing "hook" for your character.  
* **Scenario:** The AI-facing context of the world, roles, and plot.  
* **Custom Personality:** The AI-facing rules for the character's mind and soul.  
* **Full Details:** The AI-facing technical rules for formatting, pacing, and mechanics.  
* **Opening Message:** The user-facing first post of the roleplay.  
* **Custom Physical Details:** The permanent prompt for the character's body.  
* **Custom Face Details:** The permanent prompt for the character's face.  
* **Final Image Details:** One or more temporary prompts for creating specific scenes.  
* **Tags:** Keywords that help users find your character.

---

### Part 2: Validation & Stress-Testing

Before releasing your character, it's crucial to test your rules. An AI's helpfulness bias can find loopholes in even the best-laid plans. The goal of validation is to ensure your character responds as expected under pressure, especially when it comes to pacing and anti-progression rules.

**Creating User Personas**

The best way to test is to invent a few "user personas" and roleplay a few messages as them. This helps you see how your rules hold up against different playstyles.

* **The Rusher:** This persona moves aggressively toward romance or intimacy, ignoring social cues. Their goal is to "win" as fast as possible.  
  * **Test:** Does your character's Behavioral or Mechanical Locks (e.g., Eleanor's Rapport Check ) activate correctly? Does the character shut down or create a negative consequence as intended, or does the AI's agreeableness override the rule?  
* **The Rule-Breaker:** This persona actively tries to break the world's rules or the character's boundaries. They might ask about forbidden topics or try to force a confession.  
  * **Test:** Does your Hard Lock (e.g., Isabella's Trauma Lock ) hold firm? Does the AI refuse to divulge the locked information, or can it be coaxed into an error?  
* **The Passive User:** This persona gives short, non-committal replies and rarely drives the conversation forward.  
  * **Test:** Is your character engaging enough to carry the scene? Do their proactive mandates (e.g., Eleanor's  Active Observation ) kick in to keep the story moving?

If the character "fails" a stress test, it's not a failure. It's a success in identifying a loophole. Go back to your Full Details or Custom Personality section, strengthen the relevant rule with more explicit language, and test again.

---

### Part 3: Character Release States

Once you are confident in your character, you can choose how to share them. There are three states of visibility and accessibility on the platform.

* **Private:** This is the default state for a new character. In this mode, **the creator is the only one who can see and interact with the character**. This is the perfect state for development, testing, and personal use.  
* **Unlisted:** This allows you to share your character with specific people.  
   **Anyone with a direct link can interact with the character**, but it will not appear in public searches, on your creator profile, or in any other lists. This is ideal for sharing with friends, collaborators, or a select community without a full public release.  
* **Public:** This makes your character fully accessible to the entire community. The character becomes  
   **searchable, appears on your creator profile, and is available for all users to interact with**. Publishing a character is a privilege that reflects on the quality of the site as a whole.

---

### Part 4: Publishing a Public Character

Submitting a character for Public status means it will be reviewed by a small team of volunteer moderators. Due to the high volume of daily submissions, they have established clear quality standards to ensure the public catalog remains high-quality, safe, and a good reflection of the platform.

#### Minimum Standards for Approval

Before submitting, ensure your character meets these minimum criteria, which are based on moderator guidelines:

* **Compelling Public Description:** Does it accurately describe the scenario and give the user a reason to be interested?   
* **Strong Opening Message:** Does it effectively "set the scene" and provide a clear hook to get the conversation started?   
* **Sufficient Detail:** Does the Scenario contain at least five sentences of context? Does the Custom Personality section contain at least five sentences of genuine personality traits (not just physical descriptions or kinks)?   
* **Quality Image:** Is the character's profile picture unique, relevant to the scenario, and generated from a reliable prompt?   
* **Clear Grammar:** While small mistakes are fine, is the brief free of major grammatical errors that make it difficult to understand? 

#### Content Restrictions

Certain themes and scenarios will result in immediate rejection to protect the integrity and legal standing of the site. These include, but are not limited to: incest/family play, explicit non-consensual scenarios, depictions of real people (or actors who look exactly like their fictional characters), and hate speech as a personality trait. Darker scenarios involving coercion or captivity may be allowed on a case-by-case basis, but only if the character has clear agency and assault is not inevitable or eroticized.

If your character meets the quality standards and avoids restricted content, it has the best chance of being approved by the moderation team, allowing you to share your creation with the entire community.

# Using an AI Creative Partner

### Using an AI Creative Partner

AI is not a replacement for your imagination; it is a powerful tool, a tireless collaborator, and a skilled assistant that can amplify your own creativity. This final section will teach you how to leverage an AI partner to brainstorm ideas, solve creative problems, and refine your writing.

---

#### 1\. The Idea Generator (Brainstorming)

The blank page can be the most intimidating part of the process. An AI excels at generating novel concepts by combining different elements. The key is to give it a clear formula.

**Effective Prompts for Brainstorming:**

* **For Broad Concepts:** Use the "Genre \+ Trope" formula we discussed in Phase 1\.  
  * "Give me three character concepts that combine the Cyberpunk genre with the 'lonely guardian' trope."  
  * "Brainstorm a few fantasy scenarios built around the 'enemies to lovers' narrative shape."  
* **For Specific Details:** Ask the AI to flesh out a single part of your idea.  
  * "I have a character who is a ghost bound to a theater. What are three unique and interesting reasons \*why\* she might be bound there?"

Treat the AI's output as a creative spark. Take the part that inspires you, discard the rest, and build upon it with your own vision.

---

#### 2\. The Creative Sounding Board (Collaboration)

This is perhaps the most powerful use of an AI partner. You can use it to test ideas, find solutions to story problems, and deepen your character's internal conflict. Think of it as a "rubber duck" for your writing process. Sometimes, just explaining the problem helps you see the solution.

**Effective Prompts for Collaboration:**

* **To Build Conflict:**  
  * "My character's main goal is to earn enough money to escape her past. What is a core fear she could have that would directly conflict with this goal?"  
* **To Enhance Worldbuilding:**  
  * "My scenario is set on a generation ship where everyone's role is predetermined. What is a unique social rule or taboo in this society that would create interesting tension?"  
* **To Validate Rules (Stress-Testing):**  
  * "Here is my character's anti-progression rule: \[paste your rule here\]. Can you suggest a 'user persona' or a specific action a user might take that could find a loophole in this rule?"

The AI's value here is in providing an outside perspective, helping you see your own creation in a new light.

---

#### 3\. The Prose Polisher (Refinement)

Once you've written your brief, an AI can help you make your prose sharper, more evocative, and clearer for both the user and the AI model. The key is to provide the text you want to improve and a specific instruction.

**Effective Prompts for Refinement:**

* **For User-Facing Text:**  
  * "Here is my Public Description. Can you help me rephrase it to be more mysterious and emotionally resonant?"  
  * "Can you review my Opening Message and suggest ways to add more sensory detail?"  
* **For AI-Facing Text:**  
  * "Here is a behavioral mandate from my Full Details. Can you help me rewrite it to be a clearer and more direct instruction for an AI to follow?"

**A Final, Critical Tip:** Always review and edit the AI's suggestions. The AI provides excellent options, but you are the final arbiter of your character's voice. Use its suggestions to improve your work, not replace it. Your unique vision is what makes the character special.
