# Ash Harbor – Official Public Introduction (English)
> Word count: approx. 970

Three years have passed since the Silence. The event that erased most of humanity in a matter of weeks did not come with fire or radiation or marching machines. It came quiet, and by the time anyone understood what was happening, the cities had already gone dark: no power grid, no communications, no convoys on the highways, no cargo ships on the horizon. Ash Harbor — a mid-sized industrial port city of half a million on the Soviet-era Pacific coast — became, almost overnight, a half-ruin. A skeleton. A place where salt wind, rust, and silence are the background noise of every day.

Today, roughly 300 people still live inside its grid of nine districts. Scavenger peaks have passed; modern stockpiles still exist, but the valuable, sealed, and usable caches are shrinking week by week. There is no mayor, no police, no reconstruction council, no unified currency. What passes for order instead rests, barely, on bare threat and raw utility: six loose factions, each clinging to the one resource stream they can defend against the others.

The Water Commerce Syndicate, headquartered in the old city hall, controls Central and East — collecting stall fees, auction commissions, and "shelter dues" with a quiet, methodical brutality no one can yet afford to openly defy. The Northeast Farm Collective works the ruined arable belt behind the walls of a converted prison, trading grain for tools and ammunition, always wary of the Syndicate pushing its "broker guarantees" onto every harvest route that crosses south. The Dock Gang runs what remains of the piers and fisheries, loaded guns and loaded nets moving side by side. The Coal Crew digs the western tailings for what the city needs to keep warm through the killing winters. Scavengers crawl through every unmarked closet, warehouse, and collapsed factory floor. And the Reaver Brotherhood takes what it wants, when it wants — as long as the target cannot shoot back well enough.

This is not a game about quests. This is not a game about hand-holding tutorials, safe starter towns, or quest-givers with yellow exclamation marks floating above their heads. There is no safe zone in Ash Harbor. No one will tell you where to go next. No one will give you free information. Every person you meet — the Syndicate tax collector, the Farm hand carrying sacks of potatoes, the Dock crewman smoking by a rusted hull, the miner with scars on his knuckles, the Reaver watching you from a rooftop at dusk — cares first and only about his own survival, his own fears, his own debts, his own small circle of people worth keeping alive.

Non-player characters in Ash Harbor are not system mouthpieces. They know only four things: what they have seen with their own eyes, what they have just received from a trusted runner, what their job and their life experience allow them to guess, and what they cannot confirm at all. They will not solve your problems for you. They will not give you a ten-step strategic overview of the whole city. They will lie, omit, hedge, and deflect. They will sell you information they themselves think might be wrong if the price is right. They will not warn you about a trap unless your death would cost them something they value.

Four rough paths keep you alive, and every one of them extracts a price. Scavenge for what is left — but scavenging makes noise, takes time, draws attention, and the inventory of the dead and fled city does not grow back. Trade what you can carry — tinned food and rounds of ammunition are the only hard money anyone trusts, and if you walk into a market with nothing, you will leave with less than nothing. Produce: plant, fish, forage, mend, smelt, brew, carve, filter — slow, steady, vulnerable to weather, disease, and anyone who figures out where your plots and traps and stills are. Or fight. Take. Threaten, betray, raid, steal, burn bridges, and break faces. Violence works here. It usually also produces an exacting, long-term cost — one that will come looking for you when you least expect it.

Five invisible rails press on every minute of every day, and ignoring them is the fastest way to die of something that did not even involve a gun. Fatigue from walking, carrying too much, not sleeping, fighting through the night. Body temperature — Ash Harbor's winters are cold and wet, and cold + wet + tired + hungry kills faster than any knife. Dehydration — raw river water is a gamble, and shortages bite before hunger does. Hunger — slow, grinding, worsening every judgment, every wound, every shiver. And injury — not just "hit points" draining away, but the way a broken arm ruins your aim, slows your pace, ruins your sleep, and makes it impossible to stay warm. Recovery is always slower than collapse. A short rest might stop you from getting worse. To actually get better, you need dry, warm, safe, full, clean water — and in Ash Harbor, almost no one has all six of those at the same time.

The city is carved into a nine-district grid. Northwest, North, Northeast; Industrial, Central, East; Southwest, South, Southeast. The underground pipe network is dead and clogged; there is no stable tunnel shortcut. Movement between zones has real routes, real step counts, real travel time. Routing over the bridge in winter means wind exposure. Walking through Industrial after dark means a higher chance of contact. Going around a checkpoint might save you a tax — and cost you two hours and a chance encounter with a group that does not feel like asking questions first.

What Ash Harbor is, at its core, is a place where the story emerges from the costs. There is no written main plot you are supposed to follow. There are no scripted betrayals or pre-arranged "best endings." There is only the city. The factions. The cold. The dwindling caches. The five rails pushing down on every decision. The people you meet — what they want, what they fear, what they will trade for, what they will kill for. The shelter you build, the debts you owe, the grudges you accumulate, the allies you trust enough to sleep near. And a single question that every single player answers differently:
What are you willing to give up — and who are you willing to become — to make it through one more winter in Ash Harbor?

# 灰港（Ash Harbor）官方公开介绍（中文翻译）
> 约 1200 字

**寂灭已过去三年。**

那场在数周内抹去绝大多数人类的事件，没有伴随火焰、辐射，或行军的机械。它来得无声无息，等有人意识到发生了什么，城市已经陷入黑暗：电网断了，通讯停了，公路上没有车队，地平线上也不再有货轮的影子。**灰港（Ash Harbor）**——一座苏联时代太平洋沿岸的中型工业港口城市，灾变前人口约五十万——几乎在一夜之间，变成了一座半废墟、一副空骨架。在这里，咸风、铁锈和寂静，是每一天的背景音。

今天，九宫格的城市版图里还活着约三百余人。**拾荒的高峰已过**：现代遗留的封存物资仍有，但高价值、可使用的库存每周都在减少。没有市长，没有警察，没有重建委员会，也没有统一货币。勉强称得上"秩序"的东西，仅靠赤裸裸的威慑与赤裸的效用维持：六个松散的阵营，各自死死守住一条自己能打得过别人守住的资源命脉。

**水源商会**以旧市政厅为据点，控着中心与东区——摊位费、拍卖抽成、"据点费"，用一种无人能公开反抗的、冷静而有条不紊的暴力，维持着极薄的一层秩序。**东北农场**以改造后的监狱为墙，耕种着仅剩的可耕带，用粮食换工具与弹药，同时时刻警惕着商会把每一条南下运粮路线，都套上"代卖担保"的绞索。**码头帮**管着残余的泊位与渔业，上了膛的枪与下了水的网并肩同行。**煤矿队**在西郊尾矿坑里刨着这座城市熬过寒冬所需的煤。**拾荒者**钻进每一间无主的壁橱、仓库与塌了半边的厂房。而**劫掠者兄弟会**想要什么就拿什么——只要对方的还击火力还没猛到让他们觉得不划算。

**这不是一个关于"任务"的游戏。**

灰港没有新手教学、没有安全主城，也没有头顶黄色感叹号的任务发布者。**这里没有安全区**。没有人会告诉你下一步该去哪。没有人会免费给你信息。你遇到的每一个人——商会的收税人、农场扛土豆的雇工、码头靠在生锈船壳上抽烟的船员、指节上留着伤疤的矿工、黄昏时在屋顶盯着你的劫掠者——首先且永远只关心自己的生存、自己的恐惧、自己的债、以及自己那一小圈值得让他拼命活下去的人。

灰港里的 NPC **不是系统传声筒**。他们只知道四件事：亲眼看到的、可信跑腿刚送来的、职业与经验允许他们去猜的、以及根本无法确认的。他们不会替你解决问题。不会给你一份十条的全局战略总览。他们会撒谎、会省略、会打太极、会兜圈子。价格合适时，他们会把自己都吃不准的消息当真消息卖给你。除非你的死会让他们损失他们真正在乎的东西，否则他们不会提醒你前面是个陷阱。

**四条粗糙的活路能让你活下来，每一条都要你付代价。**

搜刮——翻找那些剩余的东西：但搜刮有噪音、费时间、会引人注意，而这座死去与逃亡之城的库存，**不会再长出来**。

交易——用你能背得动的东西去换：**罐头和弹药是唯一所有人都认的硬通货**，你空着手进市场，出来时欠的只会比进来时更多。

生产——种、捕、采、补、炼、酿、雕、滤：慢、稳、但靠天吃饭，靠病吃饭，靠不被人发现你的菜地、陷坑、蒸馏炉、打铁炉吃饭。

或冲突。抢。威胁、背叛、夜袭、偷窃、烧桥、动手。暴力在这里有用。但暴力通常也会留下一笔极其精确、长期有效、并且专挑你最脆弱的时候上门来收的账。

**五条看不见的轨道，每一分钟都在压在你身上。** 无视它们，是最快死于一种甚至连枪都没用上的死法的方式。**疲劳**——走太远、背太重、不睡、连夜战斗；**体温**——灰港的冬天又冷又湿，而**冷 + 湿 + 累 + 饿**，比任何一把刀杀人都快；**脱水**——生水是赌局，缺水上限永远比饥饿先到；**饥饿**——缓慢、磨人，放大每一次战栗、每一道伤口、每一个错误判断；以及**伤病**——不是"掉血点"那么抽象：断了的胳膊会烂掉你的瞄准、拖垮你的步速、毁了你的睡眠、让你根本不可能暖起来。

恢复永远比崩溃慢。短暂的休息也许能止住恶化。真要好起来——你需要干燥、温暖、安全、吃饱、干净的水——在灰港，几乎没有人能同时凑齐这六件。

城市被切成九宫格分区。西北 / 北 / 东北；工业 / 中心 / 东；西南 / 南 / 东南。地下管网早已堵塞报废，没有稳定的地下捷径。**跨区移动有真实的路线、真实的步数、真实的耗时。** 冬天走桥面要承受海风暴露。入夜穿工业区，意味着更高的接触概率。绕开一个关卡也许能省下税——但会让你多走两个小时，并且撞见一队不想先问问题的人。

灰港的核心魅力，在于**故事从代价里自然生长**。

没有预先写好、等着你去走的主线剧情。没有脚本写好的背叛，也没有预设好的"最佳结局"。只有这座城市。这些阵营。寒冷。日渐枯竭的物资仓库。每一个决定下都压着的五条生存轨道。你遇见的人——他们想要什么，他们怕什么，他们愿意拿什么换什么，他们愿意为什么杀人。你搭起来的庇护所。你欠下的账。你种下的仇。你能放心地睡在他旁边的盟友。以及每一个玩家都会给出不同答案的那个问题——

**你愿意放弃什么？你愿意变成谁？只为了，在灰港，再多熬过一个冬天。**
